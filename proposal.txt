This proposal is governed by the following precedence relation:

$$
\boxed{
\text{explicit user statements}
\succ
\text{intent necessarily implied by them}
\succ
\text{broad RRA and Factor architecture}

A lower-precedence source may clarify a higher-precedence source but may not override it.

# 1. Corrections to the previous proposal

The previous proposal had the correct central direction:

$$
\boxed{
\text{RRA supplies structure}
\qquad
\text{Factor supplies semantics}
}
$$

It also correctly rejected an independent state-machine or effect semantics and placed the initial normative definitions in Isabelle/HOL.

It nevertheless made several decisions that were not justified.

First, it treated the RRA live-head map as though it were necessarily an ambient mutable state. That is not required. A live state and a transaction may be defined as finite mathematical values and a pure relation between them. The implementation may mutate storage, but no mutation operation needs semantic force.

Second, it suggested that the RRA assembly map \(q\) should replace Factor’s fresh-position map \(\alpha\). That may ultimately be correct, but it must be proved from the exact integrated representation. The two maps cannot be identified merely because both mention output positions.

Third, it proposed replacing Factor’s retained proof envelope with the RRA Evidence Envelope. This collapsed two distinct functions:

* RRA Evidence records exact linkage;
* Factor replay determines whether retained structure realizes a valid derivation.

They should be connected, not identified.

Fourth, it proposed collapsing Factor frontiers and RRA trajectories. The current materials use them at different scopes:

* Factor frontiers account for admitted occurrences and causal availability inside a fixed model;
* RRA trajectories account for succession between exact generations.

They may later admit a derivation relation, but they are not presently the same fact.

Fifth, it suggested eliminating or structurally expanding RRA data profiles. The supplied intent determines that data has no intrinsic semantics, but it does not determine whether the four existing structural data profiles are minimal or redundant.

Finally, it introduced several new named relations where existing Factor relations or structurally recognized applications should have been used. The revised proposal introduces no primitive solely to obtain convenient terminology.

# 2. Admission condition for every foundational distinction

The complete system is intended to be irredundant and sufficient. Those terms impose a condition on every proposed primitive, field, relation, and layer.

A normative distinction \(d\) may be admitted only when all three conditions hold.

## 2.1 Sufficiency

The complete supplied boundary determines every judgment that depends on \(d\).

There must be no hidden input from:

* names;
* host types;
* registries;
* evaluator modes;
* parser choices;
* storage locations;
* current process state;
* omitted-field conventions;
* lookup order;
* an ambient “latest version.”

For each public judgment \(J\), there must be a finite boundary \(\partial J\) such that agreement on \(\partial J\) preserves formation and truth:

$$
M\!\restriction_{\partial J}
=
M'\!\restriction_{\partial J}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\bigl(
\operatorname{Formed}_{M}(J)
\leftrightarrow
\operatorname{Formed}_{M'}(J)
\bigr)
$$

and, whenever formed,

$$
M\models J
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
M'\models J.
$$

## 2.2 Irredundancy

A distinction is not primitive when it is uniquely derivable from earlier structure.

If two stored fields always determine one another, only one is normative. The other may be a derived projection.

If a proposed primitive can be removed without making two required cases structurally indistinguishable, it must be removed.

Conversely, two relations must not be collapsed merely because their values sometimes coincide. They may be identified only after proving that each is uniquely recoverable from the other over the complete intended domain.

## 2.3 Necessity

Removing the distinction must prevent some required structural or semantic case from being represented or distinguished.

Convenience, familiar terminology, implementation efficiency, or resemblance to an existing programming language does not establish necessity.

The intended admission test is therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Admit}(d)
\iff
\operatorname{SufficientBoundary}(d)
\land
\operatorname{NotDerivableEarlier}(d)
\land
\operatorname{Required}(d)
}
$$

This is a design criterion to be formalized in the Isabelle development where applicable. It is not itself proposed as an additional semantic primitive.

# 4. \(\mathsf{R}_0\): finite structure without domain meaning

An RRA structure is:

$$

\qquad
I\subseteq U\times U\times U,
$$

with finite \(U\) and \(I\).

For

$$
(r,p,x)\in I,
$$

the three positions have only their minimal structural readings:

* \(r\) is the relation occurrence;
* \(p\) is the participation occurrence;
* \(x\) is the reached occurrence.

These are positions in incidence, not intrinsic sorts.

No primitive distinction is made between:

* objects and relations;
* values and types;
* programs and data;
* terms and proofs;
* entities and metadata;
* structures and descriptions of structures.

Any such distinction is additional structure interpreted by Factor.

A formed RRA structure may contain any finite incidence pattern, including self-incidence, cycles, sharing, and relations whose reached occurrences are themselves relation or participation occurrences.

## 4.1 Bounded views

A bounded view

$$
(S,b)
$$

distinguishes selected atoms for one use of \(S\).

The keys of \(b\):

* belong to the external statement of the view;
* do not become stored properties of the selected atoms;
* are fixed when comparing that view;
* may be replaced by a structurally represented interface when the definition is reflected internally.

A bounded view is therefore the initial metalevel mechanism by which a structural predicate exposes its arguments without assigning permanent kinds to them.

## 4.2 No structural fact implies domain semantics

RRA formation establishes only that the structural object is formed.

In particular:

$$
\operatorname{Formed}_{RRA}(S)
\not\Rightarrow
\operatorname{Type}(S),
$$

$$
\operatorname{Formed}_{RRA}(S)
\not\Rightarrow
\operatorname{True}(S),
$$

and:

$$
\operatorname{Formed}_{RRA}(S)
\not\Rightarrow
\operatorname{Program}(S).
$$

These notions may be defined by Factor, but none is recoverable from RRA formation alone.

$$

An implementation may use locks, compare-and-swap, consensus, append-only storage, or another mechanism. Those procedures do not define `Transact`.

* idempotent;
* monotone;
* eventually consistent;

is a Factor theorem about that protocol, not a built-in consequence of trajectory formation.

### Publication

A publication is an exact finite snapshot of selected locus-generation pairs and the exact dependencies, evidence, and authority material selected with them.

It performs no ambient live lookup after formation.

# 10. Factor as the programming language

“Program” is not a new primitive sort.

A program is a Factor semantic definition whose formed application boundary exposes the structure needed to determine an output construction.

A program application is an ordinary formed Factor judgment. Its program target, inputs, construction parameters, and claimed output are recovered from the complete structural boundary.

The central programming relation remains Factor application. No separate RRA virtual-machine semantics is introduced.

## 10.1 Computation

A computation is the existence of a finite structurally accounted application:

$$
\exists W.\;
\operatorname{Apply}_{\Phi}(J,W).
$$

The witness \(W\) includes the exact construction account required by the application.

An implementation “runs” the program by attempting to construct such a witness and result.

The search strategy has no semantic force.

Consequently:

* failure to find a result does not prove nonexistence;
* different strategies may find different valid results when the relation is nondeterministic;
* determinism is a theorem about the Factor relation;
* totality is a theorem about the Factor relation;
* termination is a theorem about a selected construction procedure or a structurally defined decreasing derivation.

## 10.2 No state or effect semantics

There is no foundational relation of the form:

$$
(\text{program},\text{machine state})
\longrightarrow
\text{machine state}'.
$$

There is no primitive effect operation.

A long-lived computation may be represented by a trajectory of exact partial constructions, claims, results, or derivations. Each generation is an immutable structure. The relation between generations is defined by Factor over the RRA trajectory.

An external observation enters as an exact artifact with provenance and any adopted interpretation. An intended external action is represented as an exact publication or claim. The physical realization between them is not assigned semantic force by the foundation.

This does not prevent software from interacting with the world. It prevents the physical realization mechanism from defining the program’s formal meaning.

## 10.3 Computational sufficiency

# 14. Authority and interpretation

Authority is a Factor semantic protocol over exact RRA structure.

It may determine that a specific authority structure:

* adopts a claim;
* selects a version;
* authorizes a continuation;
* publishes a release;
* adopts an internal-to-external interpretation.

Authority does not determine truth merely by selection.

Proof does not determine adoption merely by validity.

An exact structural correspondence does not determine external interpretation merely by existing.

Accordingly:

$$
\operatorname{Adopts}_{\Phi}(A,J)
\not\Rightarrow
\Phi\models J

unless a separately defined authority protocol includes and justifies that implication.

Likewise:

$$
\Phi\models J
\not\Rightarrow
\operatorname{Adopts}_{\Phi}(A,J).
$$

A usable adopted result may require both, but that is a conjunction rather than an identification.

## 15.1 Theory dependency must mirror semantic dependency

The Isabelle theories should follow the same graph as the proposed foundation:

$$
\begin{array}{ccccc}
\mathsf{R}_0
&\longrightarrow&
\mathsf{R}_X
&\longrightarrow&
\{\mathsf{R}_A,\mathsf{R}_E,\mathsf{R}_T\}
\\
\downarrow&&&&\downarrow\\
\mathsf{F}_{\mathrm{form}}
&\longrightarrow&
\mathsf{F}_{\mathrm{meaning}}
&\longrightarrow&
\{\mathsf{F}_{\mathrm{apply}},
  \mathsf{F}_{\mathrm{derive}},
  \mathsf{F}_{\mathrm{continuation}}\}
\\
&&&&\downarrow\\
&&&&
\mathsf{Reflection}
\end{array}
$$

A later theory may use an earlier relation. An earlier theory may not depend on proof, replay, authority, implementation, or reflection defined later.

In particular:

* RRA formation does not depend on Factor;
* Factor meaning does not depend on proof;
* derivation does not depend on retained realization;
* replay may depend on derivation and evidence;
* implementation correctness may depend on all normative relations;
* no normative relation depends on implementation behavior.

This reflects the existing Factor boundary, under which implementation devices such as encodings, indexes, and caches have no semantic or replay force. 

## 15.2 Reification

Once the external Isabelle definitions are complete, the foundation is represented as exact RRA artifacts.

The reflected foundation must contain structural representations sufficient to recover:

* RRA formation and exactness;
* assembly formation;
* evidence formation;
* trajectory formation;
* Factor structural recognition;
* Factor semantic definitions;
* application;
* derivation;
* replay;
* continuation and authority.

It need not initially internalize all of Isabelle/HOL. It must internalize the exact finite foundation required to interpret and check the system’s own public judgments.

## 15.3 No self-authorization

Let the reflected genesis foundation be \(\Phi_0\).

The fact that \(\Phi_0\) describes RRA and Factor does not make it normative.

Its initial authority comes from Isabelle theorems establishing correspondence between:

* the external Isabelle definitions;
* the exact RRA representation;
* the internal Factor interpretation.

For every public bootstrap judgment \(J\), the required adequacy theorem has the form:

$$
\boxed{
J_{\mathrm{HOL}}(x)
\iff
\operatorname{ReflectedJudgment}_{\Phi_0}
  (\ulcorner x\urcorner)
}
$$

over the exact declared representation domain.

Separate theorems must establish:

* formation preservation and reflection;
* exact-boundary preservation;
* soundness of reflected derivations;
* completeness where claimed;
* agreement of finite checkers;
* absence of hidden external arguments.

Only then may \(\Phi_0\) be used as the internal normative reference for later Factor artifacts.

## 15.4 The genesis publication

The genesis publication must fix:

* the exact reference to \(\Phi_0\);
* every exact dependency required to interpret it;
* the exact bootstrap Isabelle session identity;
* the exact adequacy results;
* the authority relation under which it is adopted;
* its genesis trajectory generation.

The Isabelle presentation and \(\Phi_0\) do not constitute two competing meanings. The adequacy theorem establishes that they are two exact presentations of one bootstrap meaning over the stated domain.

## 15.5 Later foundation generations

A later foundation \(\Phi_1\) is a new exact generation.

It must not alter the meaning of artifacts interpreted under \(\Phi_0\).

Every semantic artifact therefore identifies its exact foundation version.

A proposed successor must supply:

* its exact predecessor;
* an assembly account of reused and introduced foundation structure;
* evidence for every retained and changed dependency;
* a Factor derivation under the predecessor’s continuation rules;
* an authority decision selecting the successor.

A later stratum must not be admitted until the obligations connecting it to its predecessors have been proved.

* equality and isomorphism laws;
* bounded-view preservation;
* renaming invariance;
* exact identity distinct from isomorphism;

* exact resolution of every reference and citation;
* complete envelope boundary;
* absence of hidden semantic force;

# 18. The resulting system

The completed foundation has one structural basis and one semantic language.

$$
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{domain theories and software}\\
\downarrow\\
\textbf{Factor}\\
\text{formation, meaning, application, derivation, replay, authority}\\
\downarrow\\
\textbf{RRA}\\
\text{incidence, exact identity, assembly, evidence, trajectory}
\end{array}}
$$

This diagram does not mean that Factor merely reads RRA data.

Factor definitions, programs, applications, derivations, evidence realizations, authorities, and foundation versions are themselves exact RRA structures.

Nor does it mean that RRA structures carry Factor meaning intrinsically.

Their meaning exists only under exact Factor definitions and complete application boundaries.

The three central integration laws are:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{construction}
&=
\text{assembly}
\land
\text{Factor admissibility},
\\
\text{certified retention}
&=
\text{evidence formation}
\land
\text{Factor derivation}
\land
\text{realization},
\\
\text{meaningful continuation}
&=
\text{trajectory transition}
\land
\text{Factor continuation}.
\end{aligned}}
$$

The constitutional invariant is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{structure never silently acquires semantics,
and semantics never depends on structure that is not explicitly present.}
}
$$

The corresponding completeness invariant is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{every result has an exact structural origin,
every claim has an exact semantic boundary,
every retained justification has an exact evidence boundary,
and every continuation has an exact predecessor boundary.}
}
$$
